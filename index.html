How it works

From sensor signal to maintenance decision

The five stages of a condition monitoring workflow.

STAGE 01 · SENSE

Data acquisition

Vibration, current and operational data are collected from motors, drives, batteries and building systems, using test rigs, public benchmark datasets and partner sites.

  • Vibration
  • Motor current
  • Battery cycling
  • HVAC data

STAGE 02 · PREPARE

Signal preprocessing

Signals are filtered, segmented and converted into representations such as spectra, scalograms or raw sequences.

  • Spectra
  • Scalograms
  • Windowing

STAGE 03 · LEARN

Model training

Hybrid CNN, transformer and LSTM models are trained to recognise fault patterns, and transfer learning extends them to new machines with limited labelled data.

  • CNN
  • Transformer
  • LSTM
  • Transfer learning

STAGE 04 · DIAGNOSE

Fault diagnosis and health estimation

The models identify the fault type and severity, and for batteries estimate state of health and remaining useful life.

  • Fault class
  • State of health
  • Remaining useful life

STAGE 05 · ACT

Maintenance planning

Results support planned maintenance through early warnings with low false-alarm rates.

  • Planned maintenance
  • Low false alarms

Research areas

What we work on

Machines and batteries degrade in ways that show up in their sensor data. The lab develops methods to detect these changes early and reliably.

Induction motors · bearings · drives

Rotating machinery diagnosis

Bearing and rotor faults appear in vibration and current signals well before a motor fails. We develop deep learning models that detect these signatures, including under the limited-data conditions found in industry.

  • KEY PAPERS

State of health · remaining useful life

Battery health & e-mobility

Mobility batteries degrade non-linearly and unevenly across cells. We estimate state of health and remaining useful life from operational data, combining convolutional, transformer and recurrent architectures.

Why it matters

Early detection reduces unplanned downtime.

Condition monitoring is useful when it supports a maintenance decision before a failure occurs. The lab's methods are designed around three requirements.

  1. 01

    Early detection

    Faults are identified while there is still time to plan maintenance.

  2. 02

    Reliable accuracy

    Methods are validated on public benchmark datasets and compared with published results.

  3. 03

    Low false-alarm rates

    Alerts are kept specific so that maintenance teams can rely on them.
